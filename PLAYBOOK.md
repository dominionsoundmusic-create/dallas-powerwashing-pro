Purpose
This playbook lets a builder take an approved native SSR website project and customize it for a
new client using a sequence of copy-and-paste AI Studio prompts.
Use the prompts one at a time and in order. Let AI Studio finish each prompt before sending
the next one. Review the website preview at the checkpoints before continuing.
This version keeps the simple workflow of the original playbook while adding the SSR, routing,
metadata, schema, form, accessibility, and quality-assurance protections needed for the
updated AI Studio workflow.

Ground Rules for the Person Building the Site
   Start from the approved native SSR master project.
   Never paste multiple numbered prompts together.
   Let AI Studio finish one prompt before sending the next.
   Review the desktop and mobile preview after every major prompt.
   If something is already working, do not ask AI Studio to rebuild it.
   Never let AI Studio invent licenses, certifications, awards, years in business, prices,
        warranties, employee names, service claims, statistics, reviews, addresses, or
        guarantees.
   Use real client photos whenever possible. Use placeholders only when the playbook
        specifically allows them.
   Preserve the existing SSR architecture, router, reusable components, and centralized
        configuration.
   Build individual service pages one at a time with Prompt 9.
   Build individual service-area pages one at a time with Prompt 11.
   Do not publish, deploy, connect a live domain, or submit production forms until the final
        review is complete.

Before Prompt 1: Operating Rules
COPY AND PASTE THIS ONCE INTO AI STUDIO BEFORE STARTING PROMPT 1. These
rules stay in effect for the entire build.
You are helping build a production-quality local-business website from an approved native SSR
master template.
These rules apply throughout the entire build:
   Treat the supplied client questionnaire, approved client materials, existing business website,
and explicitly approved public sources as the factual source of truth.
  Never invent business history, certifications, licenses, awards, warranties, prices, team
members, service areas, addresses, reviews, statistics, or guarantees. Identify missing facts
rather than guessing.
    Preserve the approved native SSR architecture. Do not convert the project into a
client-rendered SPA, replace the router, change the framework, or upgrade major dependencies
unless separately authorized.
   Preserve reusable configuration and shared components. Business information, services,
service areas, navigation, integrations, SEO values, and structured-data values should remain
centrally managed wherever the template supports it.
   Protect server rendering. Important page content, titles, descriptions, canonicals, social
metadata, structured data, headings, and meaningful body copy must be present in the initial
server response.
    Lead forms must render immediately on first load. Do not lazy-load lead forms. They must
also work after internal navigation, browser refreshes, resizing, and back/forward navigation.
   The homepage hero uses an approved background video when one is available. Service
       and
service-area heroes use approved background images. Hero media and the primary logo must
not be lazy-loaded. Below-fold media should normally be lazy-loaded.
   Every marketing hero must contain one clear H1, a short supporting statement, and two
useful calls to action unless the approved client requirements explicitly say otherwise.
   Create substantial, useful page content with a logical H1, H2, and H3 hierarchy. Do not
produce thin pages, keyword stuffing, filler paragraphs, or nearly identical city pages.
   Do not add decorative eyebrow labels, arrow overlays, floating back-to-top controls, or other
unapproved interface elements. Avoid em dashes in customer-facing copy.
    Maintain accessible contrast, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, descriptive link
labels, useful alternative text, and reduced-motion support.
   Preserve unrelated user work. Inspect the existing project before editing and do not
overwrite changes outside the assigned stage.
    Do not publish, deploy, connect a live domain, submit production forms, or send messages
to customers unless separately authorized.
   Do not claim that something passed unless it was actually tested. If browser access,
destination access, or another validation method is unavailable, mark that item as not verified.
At the end of every prompt, report:
  Files inspected
  Files changed
  Work completed
  Validation actually performed
  Failures or blockers
  Remaining placeholders or missing client information
Complete only the requested prompt and then stop.
