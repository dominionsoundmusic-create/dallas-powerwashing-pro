Dallas

Dallas is the center of the line's coverage. City code gives a defense for plain wash water but prohibits mobile power washing and pavement washdown with a harmful quantity of cleaner. The home page covers Dallas in detail, and the two-day watering schedule and fines are on Save Dallas Water.

Other cities the line takes calls from

These cities have too little search data for a page of their own, but the line takes calls from all of them.

Allen and Rockwall

Both are North Texas Municipal Water District members, so the district's Stage 3 limit on hosing paved areas and buildings is the plan to know.

Garland, Richardson and Mesquite

These are district member cities too. Older homes are worth a lead paint question if they were built before 1978.

Wylie

Also a district member. As elsewhere on the prairie, clay soil makes driveway cracks a common topic.

Irving and Coppell

A mix of offices, apartments and homes. Ask your city what stage applies, and read the commercial page if you manage a property.

Lewisville and The Colony

Runoff here is close to Lewisville Lake, which Denton calls a primary drinking water source for much of the metroplex.

Grand Prairie, Mansfield and Rowlett

Say which side of town you are on and what surface you need washed, so the summary is clear.

Which water plan applies to you?

Your city's supplier often decides which drought rules matter. A few examples from city and district pages:

  • North Texas Municipal Water District members: Allen, Frisco, Garland, McKinney, Mesquite, Plano, Richardson, Rockwall and Wylie, among others.
  • Buys from Dallas: Carrollton and Addison.
  • Gets water from Fort Worth: Southlake.
  • Stages set by the Tarrant Regional Water District: Keller.

Your water bill names your provider. That is the office to call about the current stage.
