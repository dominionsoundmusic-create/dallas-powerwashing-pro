Fort Worth requires a permit for mobile power washers

Most North Texas cities regulate wash water. Fort Worth also regulates the washer. The city requires a permit for "any person or business who engages in mobile commercial cosmetic cleaning," which is the city's term for mobile pressure and power washing.

The permit

$25 for the business and $25 to register each wash unit. One year, not transferable. Vehicles and trailers must be registered, with numbers displayed on each side.

The water

No wash water off the property or into the storm drain, manholes strictly off limits, and any discharge passed through an oil absorbent boom or pad.

The penalty

Up to $2,000 per occurrence for permit or discharge violations, and illicit discharges are a criminal offense, the city says.

For a homeowner or business, that gives you an easy question to ask any company working in Fort Worth: "Can I see your city permit?"

Wash water in Fort Worth: the plain version

The city's FAQ for commercial cleaners says it has been illegal to discharge any type of wastewater off-property in Fort Worth since the turn of the century. It points washers to the sanitary sewer through a clean-out stub, sink or suitable indoor drain, or to a grit trap on site, and warns that most outdoor floor drains lead to the storm sewer. It also warns never to open a manhole, and notes that excessive dirt in storm water can break the state's standard for suspended solids.

In practice, a good company covers the curb inlet, controls where rinse water runs on your lot, and recovers it when it has used cleaners. If a company shrugs at the question, keep looking.
