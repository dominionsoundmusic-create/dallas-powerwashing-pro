Why power washing in Fort Worth has a paper trail

A written rule gives a customer something to check, and Fort Worth has one.

Under its Mobile Commercial Cosmetic Cleaning regulation, any person or business doing mobile power washing for commercial purposes must hold a city permit. It costs $25 for the business and $25 per wash unit, lasts a year and can not be transferred. Applicants give proof of insurance for vehicles and copies of driver's licenses. Every registered vehicle or trailer must show its registration number on each side, in characters at least 3 inches tall.

The rule grew out of real findings. The city says a 1993 study found detergents at more than half of 560 tested sites, making them the most common pollutant.

Three questions to ask in Fort Worth

  1. Can I see your permit or registration number?It should be on the rig. If you are hiring for a business, write it into the work order.
  2. Hot water or cold?Water above 110 degrees needs prior permission from the city to go to a storm drain, and even cold water must hold no soap, detergent or degreaser.
  3. Where will the wash water end up?The city accepts collecting it on site with a boom and sump pump or wet vacuum. A sanitary sewer discharge needs the owner's permission, can not go through a manhole, and must meet the city's limits. The city recommends a 400 micron filter.

Old Fort Worth homes need a lighter touch

The city lists 14 historic districts, including Fairmount, Mistletoe Heights, Hillcrest and the Stockyards.

Fairmount alone covers roughly 375 acres about two miles south of downtown, developed between 1885 and 1940, and its most common house is the wood-frame bungalow. Homes that old raise two issues. Their paint may contain lead, and old masonry, mortar and wood trim can be easily damaged.

The Secretary of the Interior's Standards, posted on the city's site, say physical treatments should use the gentlest means possible. Its masonry guidance lists high-pressure water as not recommended and points toward low-pressure water, detergent and soft brushes, tested on a small area first. If your home is in a district, call preservation staff before you hire.

House washing and driveways around Fort Worth

Soil here changes across town. Texas Parks and Wildlife describes the Fort Worth Prairie as thin soil over hard limestone, unlike the deep clay east of the city, so a driveway in one neighborhood may age differently from one a few miles away. Ask the company to look at the actual slab, and mention any cracks when you call. If you want house washing, say what the walls are made of, and if it is driveway cleaning, say how many cars park there.

Fort Worth's drought plan also touches this work. Stage 1 discourages hosing paved areas and buildings, and at Stage 3, pressure washing of buildings is limited to surface preparation before painting, by a professional service with high efficiency equipment. Check the stage before you book. The line takes Fort Worth calls at any hour, and you can read about driveway cleaning, house washing and commercial work before you phone. To report a discharge, the city's pollution hotline is 817-392-1234.

Stockyards buildings and business sites

The Stockyards is one of the city's listed historic districts, and old brick streets and walls call for the same gentle thinking. For businesses anywhere in town, remember that the permit is only a start. The city says a violation of permit discharge requirements can bring fines of up to $2,000 per occurrence, and that the water department can fine for going over sewer limits.
