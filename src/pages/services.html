Pick the job that sounds like yours

Every service below is carried out by an independent local company. The line connects you, and the company does the rest.

House washing

Brick, fiber cement, vinyl and painted wood each have a limit. James Hardie, for one, says to stay under 1500 psi with a wide fan tip and at least 6 feet from the wall. This is the job where the wrong setting costs the most.

{{ img('pw-05-card.jpg', 'A worker in a cap and dark uniform rinsing the front of a brick and siding house with a long wand', 620, 620) }}

Commercial power washing

Storefronts, walks, parking lots and dumpster pads. Grease and fuel residue mean more detergent and hot water, so collecting the wash water is often part of the job.

{{ img('pw-06-card.jpg', 'A worker pressure washing the sidewalk in front of a row of storefronts', 1200, 675) }}

Driveway and concrete cleaning

The toughest surface and the most forgiving, unless the slab is old or already cracked. Oil stains usually need treating before the wash.

{{ img('pw-01-card.jpg', 'A pressure washer spray fanning across a cracked concrete driveway', 1200, 675) }}

Roof soft wash

Not a pressure job at all. The roofing trade warns that high pressure is likely to damage asphalt shingles, so roofs call for a gently applied cleaning solution instead.

{{ img('pw-03-card.jpg', 'A tile roof, half dark with buildup and half clean and red', 1200, 675) }}

Fence cleaning

Wood fences go gray and grow mildew on the shady side. Washing brings back color. It can not fix a rotted picket or a leaning post.

{{ img('pw-02-card.jpg', 'A spray wand cleaning a weathered wood fence', 620, 620) }}

Deck cleaning

Softwood needs far less pressure than concrete, and composite decks have their own maker limits. Decks are often washed just before staining.

{{ img('pw-04-card.jpg', 'A wet wood deck with weathered boards and patio furniture', 620, 620) }}
