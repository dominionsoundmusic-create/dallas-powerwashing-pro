Commercial power washing in Dallas is about first impressions and liability at the same time. Gum, spilled drinks and grease on a storefront walk look bad and can make it slippery. A dumpster pad that is never cleaned draws flies and smells. And the wash water from all of it is regulated, so how a company works matters as much as how clean the concrete gets.

This is a free phone line, not a commercial cleaning company. Say it is a business job, describe the property, and an automated assistant puts you through to an independent, locally owned DFW company that does commercial work. That company walks the site, quotes it, schedules around your hours and is paid by you.

What businesses usually have cleaned

Storefront walks and entries

Gum, foot traffic, spills and pollen. Often on a recurring schedule, early morning before opening.

Drive-thru lanes

Oil drips and grime where cars idle. Usually needs degreasing, not just water.

Dumpster pads and enclosures

Food waste, grease and odor. The dirtiest job on most sites, and the one where runoff control matters most.

Building exteriors and canopies

Tilt-wall, brick, metal panel and storefront glass frames, mostly soft washed like a house.

Parking areas and garages

Oil spots, tire marks and dirt. Larger areas are often priced by the square foot; ask how your quote is figured.

Apartment and HOA common areas

Pool decks, breezeways, stairs, mail kiosks and amenity walks.

Power washing: why hot water matters on commercial jobs

"Power washing" usually means the water is heated. Angi's cost guide notes that power washing with hot water costs more than pressure washing with cold water, and NEXT Insurance's explainer points out that heated water is preferred on heavily soiled jobs with grease. On a restaurant pad or a drive-thru lane, hot water and the right degreaser do what cold water alone cannot.

The flip side is that hot, greasy water is exactly what a city does not want in its storm drains, which brings us to the rules.

Wash water rules for Dallas and Fort Worth businesses

Dallas

The City of Dallas tells businesses it is a violation of city code and the federal Clean Water Act to let water mixed with soap or other pollutants into the storm drain or street. Wash water from cleaning pavement, parking lots, driveways or structures may only go to the drain if it is uncontaminated by soaps, detergents or degreasers, will not harm the receiving creek, and does not cause erosion or muddy runoff. If cleaning chemicals are used, the drain must be blocked and all of the contaminated water collected, for example with a wet vacuum.

Fort Worth

Fort Worth goes further. It requires a permit for anyone engaged in mobile commercial cosmetic cleaning: a $25 permit for the business and a $25 registration certificate for each wash unit, good for one year and not transferable, with vehicles and trailers registered and numbered. Wash water may not leave the property or enter the storm drain, discharges must pass through an oil absorbent boom or pad, and fines can reach $2,000 per occurrence. The city's FAQ notes it has been illegal to discharge any type of wastewater off-property in Fort Worth since the turn of the century.

Other DFW cities have their own storm water ordinances. A company that does commercial work regularly should be able to explain what it does with the water on your site. If it cannot, keep looking.

What to get in writing from a commercial company

  • Proof of general liability insurance, and whether your property manager needs to be named on it.
  • The scope by area: walks, pad, lanes, building faces, with square footage or space counts.
  • Water handling: drain covers, booms, recovery, and where the water ends up.
  • Timing: hours, how long areas stay wet, signage and cones for safety.
  • The Fort Worth permit, if the work is in Fort Worth.
  • Sales tax: the Texas Comptroller treats pressure washing buildings, sidewalks and parking lots as taxable cleaning.
  • Frequency and price for recurring visits, if you want a schedule.

Building faces, canopies and roofs

A commercial building's walls are cleaned much like a house: low pressure and a cleaning solution for painted tilt-wall, brick, stucco and metal panels, and a gentler hand around storefront glass, door seals and signage. Low-slope commercial roofs have their own manufacturer rules, so roof work should only be done by a company that knows the membrane.
