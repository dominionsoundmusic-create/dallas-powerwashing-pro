Pressure washing in Dallas: why the method matters more than the machine

Anyone can rent a pressure washer. The difference between a good result and a damaged surface is knowing when not to use full pressure. The groups that write the care rules for building materials are blunt about it:

  • Brick: the Brick Industry Association warns that excessive pressure can damage brick faces and erode mortar joints, and that high-pressure water cleaning "is not appropriate for brick." Brick is everywhere in DFW, so this matters on almost every house.
  • Shingle roofs: the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association says not to use a power washer on a roof at all, because high pressure is likely to damage asphalt roofing. Black streaks are algae, and they come off with a soft wash.
  • Composite decks: Trex allows a gentle pressure wash on its newer boards only within its own limits and spray distance, and says going past them can void the warranty.

So the useful question when you talk to a company is not "how strong is your machine," it is "how will you clean each surface." The line connects you with a company that can answer that.

What North Texas weather does to the outside of a home

DFW averages about 37 inches of rain a year, according to the National Weather Service's 1991 to 2020 normals, but it rarely comes gently. Spring storms bring hail, and the Insurance Council of Texas has counted Tarrant County as the most hail-struck county in the state. After a storm, siding and walks carry a film of grit and torn leaves.

Summer is the opposite problem. The Weather Service says DFW's first 100 degree day arrives on average around July 1 and the last around August 27, and 2011 had a record 71 of them. Heat bakes dirt onto concrete and fades a fence fast. Winter can freeze hard too: in February 2021 DFW Airport hit 2 degrees below zero and stayed at or below freezing for 139 hours straight, which is why a careful company will not wash on a day that is about to freeze.

Before anyone sprays: four things to ask any company

Proof of liability insurance

Texas has no state license for pressure washing, so insurance is the real protection if a window breaks or water gets into a wall. Ask to see the certificate.

A written price for each surface

House, driveway, fence and roof priced separately, so you can compare quotes line by line and nothing changes once the work starts.

Soft wash for siding, roofs and wood

Ask which surfaces get low pressure and a cleaning solution, and which get a surface cleaner. "We just blast it" is the wrong answer for brick, shingles and cedar.

Where the wash water goes

Dallas says letting soapy wash water reach a storm drain violates city code, and Fort Worth bans wash water in its storm drains. A good company blocks the drain and plans for runoff. See the Texas rules guide.
