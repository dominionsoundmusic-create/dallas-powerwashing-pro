Lake first: where Grapevine's wash water goes

In Grapevine, pressure washing runoff can reach the same lake that helps fill your tap.

The city's stormwater plan says northern drainage flows to Lake Grapevine, with 25 miles of the lake's shoreline in Grapevine. The city has 65.3 miles of creeks as well. And about 30 percent of its drinking water comes from that same lake.

So the question of where the water goes has a direct answer in Grapevine. On a job in the northern part of town, soapy water at the curb is heading for the lake. Ask for a wash plan that uses plain water where it can, and collects anything with a cleaner in it.

Old brick on Main Street needs a soft touch

Grapevine has a designated Main Street Historic District, and its city site publishes the Secretary of the Interior's Standards.

What the standards say

Standard 7 says chemical or physical treatments, if appropriate, will be undertaken using the gentlest means possible, and treatments that cause damage to historic materials will not be used.

What the masonry guidance adds

The full federal guidance, posted by the City of Fort Worth, lists high-pressure water as not recommended on historic masonry. It suggests low-pressure water, detergent and soft brushes, tested on a small area first.

If you own or manage a building in the historic district, talk with the city's historic preservation staff before any wash. Ask what they will want to see.

Creeks in the rest of town

Not all of Grapevine drains to the lake. The city's plan also names Cottonwood Branch, Denton Creek, the Elm Fork of the Trinity, Big Bear Creek and Little Bear Creek among the waters around it. Wherever your property sits, the path is the same: from the gutter into a storm inlet and on to one of those creeks, with no treatment on the way.

For a home, a plain water rinse steered onto the lawn is usually the simple answer. For a business with grease or oil, the water should be collected. Do not count on a drain near a building to lead to a sewer, because many outdoor drains do not. Grapevine's plan lists individual residential vehicle washing and street wash water among allowable discharges, but that is not a blanket approval for commercial washing with cleaners. If you are not sure, call Public Works at 817-410-3330 before work begins.
