Plano grew fast, and its houses are now middle-aged

Plano had about 17,900 residents in 1970. By 1990 it had 128,713, and by 2000 more than 222,000, according to census counts. The 2020 census counted 285,494 people, making it the ninth-largest city in Texas and the largest in Collin County. That growth curve tells you a lot about pressure washing in Plano: much of the city was built in a twenty-year rush, so many neighborhoods are now 25 to 50 years old, with brick that has seen decades of weather, wood fences that have grayed or been replaced, and roofs that streak on the shady slopes.

Plano's water rules, in brief

Plano has been a member of the North Texas Municipal Water District since the district's founding cities joined in 1951. The city's spring and summer watering guidelines, published April 2, 2026, run April 1 through October 31:

  • Sprinkler watering no more than twice a week, on days set by whether your address ends in an even or odd number
  • No sprinklers from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  • Hand-held hoses and soaker hoses may water foundations and landscaping any day for up to two hours
  • Skip watering when it rains or runoff is excessive

The guidelines do not address pressure washing. In a drought, cities can declare stricter stages, so check plano.gov if water is short. A good company also avoids sending runoff down the street.

What Plano homes usually need

1

Brick soft wash

Decades-old brick and mortar should be washed gently with a masonry-safe cleaner, not blasted. The shaded side often needs the most attention.

2

Fences before stain

Long runs of wood privacy fence along back and side yards gray quickly in North Texas sun. Clean first, dry for several days, then stain.

3

Roof streaks

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association says black streaks are algae and that a roof should never be power washed. Roof soft washing is the answer.

4

Cracked concrete

The Dallas County Master Gardeners describe North Texas clay shrinking and cracking when dry and heaving when wet. Older Plano drives and walks show it, and cracks hold dirt.

Corporate Plano

Plano is also a headquarters city. Toyota Motor North America moved its US headquarters to Plano from California, announced in 2014, and Frito-Lay, JCPenney and Pizza Hut are based here too. Legacy West and the corporate campuses along Legacy Drive, plus retail centers across the city, mean steady demand for storefront, entry and parking area cleaning. Commercial work comes with storm water rules; see commercial power washing.

Getting around Plano, and the areas nearby

Plano is a DART member city, with Red Line stations at Downtown Plano and Parker Road since 2002. Downtown Plano's historic storefronts, rebuilt after an 1881 fire destroyed most of the original buildings, are a reminder that the city is older than its suburbs suggest.
