What Plano's code list says about pressure washing

A 2022 Dallas Morning News list of Plano's top property violations reads like a to-do list for an exterior wash.

Exterior disrepair came first. Peeling, chipped or flaking paint on exterior wood came third. Damaged, removed or missing fences came sixth. None of those is a dirt problem, and that is the point. Years of grime hide failing paint and soft boards, and a careful wash brings them into view while they are still cheap to fix.

So in Plano, think of pressure washing as an inspection you can see. Have the walls and fence cleaned, walk the property with the company, and write down what the wash uncovered.

Plano's water comes through a regional plan

Plano is one of thirteen member cities of the North Texas Municipal Water District, along with neighbors such as Allen, Frisco, McKinney and Richardson.

The district is a wholesaler and can not enforce rules on households itself, so member cities adopt its measures through their own ordinances. Its 2024 plan sets no limit on washing at Stages 1 and 2. At Stage 3, hosing and washing of paved areas, buildings, structures and windows is prohibited unless a variance is granted and a professional service using high efficiency equipment does the work.

In practice, that means a homeowner with a rented machine stops at Stage 3, while a professional with a variance may not. It is worth a call to the city if a drought is under way.

Pressure washing around Plano homes and shops

Plano sits on the same prairie clay as much of Collin County, so driveways and walks here are worth a look before anyone starts. Soapy runoff should stay out of the curb line, as it should anywhere in the metroplex. Arlington, for one, says its storm drains carry water to creeks and lakes, not to a treatment plant. Other Plano code items a wash can bring up

The same 2022 list includes grading and drainage problems, such as stagnant water or soil erosion in the right-of-way, and limbs or shrubs blocking sidewalks. Those matter on washing day too. If water ponds in one spot at the curb, tell the company, so it does not sit there. If shrubs crowd a walk, trim them first. A company can only clean what it can reach, and clear walks let a crew work without spraying your plants.

Rental owners have a further reason to book a wash. A front that looks tired is the first thing a tenant or code officer sees, and it is cheaper to deal with flaking paint early. If the house was built before 1978, raise the lead paint question before work starts.
