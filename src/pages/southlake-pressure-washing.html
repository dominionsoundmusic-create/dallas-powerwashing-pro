Why a big house changes a pressure washing job

Pressure washing in Southlake often means more of everything: more square footage, more height, and more fine materials next to one another.

On a large home, several materials may sit side by side on one wall. Stone, brick, stucco, wood accents and copper or painted metal all react differently. A single pressure setting can not suit them all, so the job becomes a series of small decisions. That is worth asking about. Have the company point to each material and say what it will use there.

Height matters too. A second-story gable needs a plan that keeps people off ladders where possible, and water pushed upward at a low angle can get behind siding. The Vinyl Siding Institute's point about angled spray holds for most lapped materials.

Questions for a larger Southlake home

  • Can you walk each material with me and say how you will treat it?
  • How will you reach upper walls, and who is working at height?
  • What protects landscape lighting, irrigation boxes and plant beds?
  • Will you keep cleaning products off natural stone unless tested first?
  • Does your insurance certificate cover this size of job?

From a Southlake gutter to Grapevine Lake

Southlake's own annual stormwater report says the city discharges into Grapevine Lake, which is listed as impaired by pH on the state's 2024 list of impaired waters.

Stormwater is not treated

The city explains that storm drains are separate from sewers, so what enters them is not treated and can carry pollutants into local waters. Its report for fiscal 2025 counts 24 illicit discharges detected and eliminated.

Ponds belong to the neighbors

Many neighborhoods here have ponds kept up by HOAs. Creeks on private property, including HOA common areas, are the owner's responsibility. Your dues pay if a pond needs help.

Long driveways and Fort Worth water

A long driveway is mostly a matter of size. Cost guides price by the square foot, so measure before you call, and review the published ranges for concrete. A decorative or stamped drive needs gentler handling than plain concrete, so mention it.

Southlake gets its drinking water from Fort Worth, whose drought plan discourages hosing paved areas at Stage 1 and limits pressure washing at Stage 3. If a drought is in the news, call Southlake customer service at (817) 748-8051 before you book. Also read about driveway cleaning and roof soft washing.

Water features, lighting and outdoor kitchens

Larger Southlake properties often have more to protect than the walls. Landscape lights, irrigation boxes, outdoor kitchens and gas grills all sit near where the spray lands. Before work starts, mark these on a simple sketch or walk them with the company. Keep water away from any outdoor outlet, and ask for covers where needed. Natural stone may react badly to some cleaners, so ask to see a small test area first and let it dry before you judge it.

If your street shares a pond, tell your HOA manager that you plan to wash, especially if soap is involved. The city maintains a pond only if it built one and accepted it as public infrastructure, so problems with most neighborhood ponds fall on the owners. A short note to the board costs nothing.
