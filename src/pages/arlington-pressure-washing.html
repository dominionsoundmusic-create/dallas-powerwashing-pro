Pressure washing in Arlington, between the two big cities

Arlington sits in Tarrant County between Dallas and Fort Worth, with 394,266 people in the 2020 census. It is best known for its entertainment district: AT&T Stadium, home of the Dallas Cowboys since 2009, Globe Life Field for the Texas Rangers, and Six Flags Over Texas, which opened in 1961. It is also a college town; the University of Texas at Arlington enrolls more than 40,000 students. All of that shows up in the kind of calls the line gets: family homes, student rentals, game-day parking areas and restaurant patios.

The line itself cleans nothing. Call, say the job is in Arlington, and an automated assistant puts you through to an independent, locally owned pressure washing company that quotes and does the work. You pay that company.

Lake Arlington is where the tap water starts

Arlington draws raw water from Lake Arlington for its Pierce-Burch Water Treatment Plant, a lake it shares with the Trinity River Authority, which also draws raw water there, according to a 2022 city announcement. The city's stormwater guidance is clear that water entering storm drains gets no treatment before it reaches local waterways, and that car wash water and other dirty runoff ends up in rivers, creeks and lakes.

The city suggests washing with plain water or with cleaners that are biodegradable, phosphate-free and water-based, and warns that phosphates feed algae. Those are good questions to put to any company working on an Arlington property.

Four Arlington reasons to make the call

1

Brick fronts

If your Arlington home has brick, the method matters. The Brick Industry Association says high-pressure water cleaning is not appropriate for brick and can erode mortar joints, so ask for a soft wash and a test patch.

2

Rentals between tenants

With a large university in town, a lot of homes and small multifamily buildings turn over each year. A clean walk, drive and siding helps a rental show well.

3

Hail in Tarrant County

A 2012 CBS Texas report, citing National Weather Service records and the Insurance Council of Texas, put Tarrant County first in the state for hail, with about 800 hailstorms since 1955. Storms leave grit on walls and walks, and the roof should be checked before any washing.

4

Business frontage

Restaurants, hotels and shops around the entertainment district need storefront and patio cleaning, often early in the morning.
