Pressure washing in Arlington and the spill rule

The City of Arlington's stormwater page is written for ordinary residents, and one line in it goes straight to the heart of this work: never hose off the driveway or street with water to clean a spill or leak.

Why so firm? Arlington keeps storm drains and sanitary sewers apart. Water from your sink reaches a treatment plant. Water at your curb goes through the nearest storm drain to creeks, streams and lakes. So a spot of motor oil hosed down the drive does not go away. It moves.

That is the difference between washing and cleaning up. A driveway with an old oil stain and a driveway with a fresh leak are two separate problems. The fresh one gets absorbed and bagged first. The company you hire should say the same.

A safe order of work for an Arlington driveway

  1. Deal with fresh spillsAbsorb them, bag the material and put it in the trash. Fort Worth's guidance, just next door, uses absorbent clay.
  2. Plan the water pathDecide where the water should run. Grass and beds are better than the gutter. Weighted boards or sandbags can steer it.
  3. Block the inlet if cleaner is usedSoapy water should be collected, for example with a wet vacuum.
  4. Wash the concreteLower pressure at cracks and joints.
  5. Check the curbSweep up what is left, so it does not go in with the next rain.

Beyond the driveway in Arlington

Arlington homes, apartments and shops all call for different care. Multifamily property managers usually want breezeways, stairs and dumpster areas done on a schedule. Homeowners tend to call about brick fronts, fences and shaded walls. Retail centers near the entertainment district want walks and entries clean on their own timetable.

Soap, buckets and where the rinse goes

Arlington's advice on washing cars carries over well to a small home job. The city suggests washing on gravel, grass or another permeable surface, and avoiding concrete or asphalt unless the runoff drains to a planted area. It says to use a trigger nozzle, to empty buckets into a sink or toilet and not the street, and to choose a low-phosphate or phosphate-free detergent.

A professional job is larger, but the reasoning is the same. Water that soaks into soil gets filtered along the way. Water that reaches concrete and then the gutter does not. If your driveway drains straight to the street, ask the company how it will catch or steer the flow. If you manage apartments, plan for several inlets on one site, and walk them with the company before you agree on a price.
