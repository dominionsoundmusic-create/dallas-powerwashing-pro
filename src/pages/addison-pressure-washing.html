Pressure washing in a small town with big frontage

Addison is a Dallas County town of 16,661 people on about 4.35 square miles of land, and it packs a lot into that space. The town says it has nearly 200 restaurants, more per capita than any other US city. Addison Airport, opened in 1957 for business aircraft, takes up about an eighth of the town, and Addison Circle Park hosts events such as Kaboom Town! and Taste Addison. The 2020 census counted 10,591 housing units in town.

So pressure washing in Addison leans commercial: restaurant patios, entries, sidewalks along Belt Line Road, apartment breezeways and pool decks, plus townhomes and single-family streets. Water from Dallas, drought rules from Addison

Addison buys its water wholesale from Dallas, and its drought contingency plan follows suit. The plan in the town's 2019 council packet lists washing buildings and paved areas as non-essential uses and prohibits hosing off paved areas, buildings or windows starting at Stage 2. Dallas's 2019 plan, for its part, prohibits washing paved areas at Stage 3 except by variance and by a professional service with high efficiency equipment.

Outside a drought, the issue is runoff. Dallas's stormwater guidance says soapy or chemical wash water must be kept out of the storm drain and collected, a fair rule for any restaurant pad in Addison.
