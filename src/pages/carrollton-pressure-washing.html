A Carrollton job starts with Dallas water

Carrollton is its own city, but its water is not its own. The city buys it from Dallas Water Utilities.

That matters for anyone planning pressure washing in Carrollton. Dallas's drought plan says that at Stage 3, hosing and washing of paved areas, buildings and other surfaces is prohibited except by variance and by a professional service using high efficiency equipment. It also speaks to its wholesale customer cities. Carrollton sets its own rules, and this site has not read its drought plan. So watch for news from Dallas as well as from your own city hall, and call Environmental Services at 972-466-3035 if a drought is in the news.

In normal times, the city's rule that touches outdoor water use is about sprinklers, which may not run between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m. from April through October. That rule is not aimed at pressure washing, but a company working on a hot day may have its own reasons to avoid those hours.

Clay, cracks and the older Carrollton driveway

Clay soil that shrinks and swells, as the Texas Water Development Board describes it, is hard on any slab, and the longer a slab has been in the ground, the more seasons it has moved through. A washed driveway in an older Carrollton neighborhood often reveals hairline cracks that dirt had hidden. That is normal, and it does not mean the wash caused them. Take a photo before the work begins so you both know what was there.

Mention what is on the walls and how many stories the building has when you call. Then read about concrete cleaning and house washing so you know what to ask. Business owners along the Interstate 35E and Bush Turnpike corridors can start with the commercial page.

A photo record protects everyone

This is the lowest-cost protection you can buy, and it takes five minutes.

On a building more than a few decades old, write down what is already there. Walk the job with your phone before work starts and photograph cracks in the slab, loose window seals, chipped paint and any stained brick. Send the pictures to the company with your request. If something looks new afterward, you both have a record, and a fair company will welcome that.

For commercial sites, the same habit helps with the wash water plan. Mark the storm inlets on a simple sketch of the property, and show where a truck can park and where a spigot is. Carrollton's non-residential watering days are encouraged as Tuesday and Friday, which has nothing to do with washing, but it is a good reminder to tell the company when your landscape sprinklers run, so a fresh clean does not get splashed with muddy water an hour later.
