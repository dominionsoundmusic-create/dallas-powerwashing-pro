Pressure washing in a city that spans three counties

Carrollton, incorporated in 1913, had 133,434 residents at the 2020 census and reaches into three counties: Dallas, Denton and Collin. About 58 percent of its homes are owner-occupied, according to census figures. It has the largest Korean community in Texas, with Koreatown Carrollton centered near Old Denton Road and the President George Bush Turnpike, and its DART Green Line stops include Downtown Carrollton, Trinity Mills and North Carrollton/Frankford.

For pressure washing, Carrollton means established neighborhoods with brick homes, fences and drives, plus a lot of busy storefronts and restaurant frontage. Homes and storefronts, side by side

At home: brick and siding soft washed rather than blasted, which the Brick Industry Association recommends for brick; drives and walks cleaned evenly; fences and decks cleaned before stain.

At work: Koreatown restaurants, shops along the main corridors and businesses near the DART stations need entries, walks and patios cleaned, often early in the day. Commercial jobs come with wash water rules, so ask any company how it keeps runoff out of the storm drain.

Runoff, plainly

The regional council of governments notes that no local government in North Central Texas treats stormwater, so whatever reaches a curb inlet in Carrollton goes to the nearest creek. Neighboring Dallas tells property owners that wash water with soap, detergent or degreaser must not reach the drain, and that it should be blocked and collected when chemicals are used. That is a sensible standard to hold any company to.
