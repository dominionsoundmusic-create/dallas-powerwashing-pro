Driveway cleaning in Dallas is the fastest way to change how a house looks from the street. A concrete slab is porous. It soaks up tire rubber, motor oil, iron from sprinkler water and the gray growth that settles in wherever the slab stays shaded and damp. A professional cleaning lifts most of that out, evenly, without the stripes a rushed job leaves.

Dallas Power Washing Pro is a free line, not a cleaning crew. Call any hour, tell the assistant how many cars the driveway holds and what the worst stains are, and you are transferred to an independent, locally owned DFW company. It quotes the driveway, books the work and is paid by you directly.

Reading the stains on your driveway

Not every dark mark is dirt, and each kind needs its own treatment before any water is sprayed. Knowing which ones you have makes the call quicker and the quote more accurate.

Even, not striped: the surface cleaner

The tell of a poor driveway job shows up the next morning in low sun: light and dark bands where a narrow spray was swept back and forth. A careful company cleans the open slab with a rotary surface cleaner, a round housing with spinning arms underneath that keeps the spray at a fixed height. The edges, the apron by the street and the strip along the garage door get a wand afterward.

That is also gentler on the concrete. Too much pressure held too close can etch the cement paste on top and expose the sand and stone beneath, which looks rough and holds dirt faster afterward.

North Texas clay and cracked driveways

The Dallas County Master Gardeners describe DFW's expansive black clay as swelling when it is wet and shrinking and cracking when it dries. Driveways sit on that soil, so many develop cracks and slightly lifted joints over the years. Cracks catch dirt, grow weeds and hold water, and a pressure washer aimed straight into them can blow out loose filler.

Point out cracks and lifted sections when the company walks the drive. Cleaning does not fix concrete, and repair is a separate trade, but a careful company works around damage instead of making it worse.

Driveways, drains and drought stages

Driveways slope to the street, and North Texas storm drains run straight to creeks without treatment. The City of Dallas tells property owners that wash water may only reach the street or a storm drain if it is plain water with no soap, degreaser or other chemical, and that when cleaning chemicals are used the storm drain should be blocked and the dirty water collected. Oil and degreaser jobs right beside a curb inlet are exactly where that matters.

Water supply matters too. Dallas's 2019 drought contingency plan treats washing driveways and sidewalks as non-essential, and at Stage 3 prohibits washing paved areas except by variance and by a professional service using high efficiency equipment. Suburbs that buy water from Dallas or the regional districts have their own plans. In a dry summer, check the stage first.

The walk, the apron and the curb

The sidewalk in front of the house and the walk to your door usually get cleaned on the same visit. They are narrower, often shaded by street trees, and tend to be greener and more slippery than the driveway itself. Mention steps, a brick border or pavers when you call: pavers need gentler handling so the joint sand stays put.

Keeping it clean longer

  • Aim sprinklers at the lawn, not the slab, to cut rust fans and damp edges.
  • Put a drip pan under a leaking car until it is fixed. Fresh oil is far easier to lift than old oil.
  • Trim low branches and shrubs that keep the slab edges in shade.
  • Ask about sealing once the slab is clean and fully dry.
