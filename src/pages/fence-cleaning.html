Why fences in Dallas look old so soon

Fence cleaning in Dallas comes down to two enemies: sun on one side and shade on the other.

The wood that faces the sun bleaches to gray. Weather and heat do that to most softwood left unsealed. The side that stays shaded and damp grows mildew and algae, helped by humid summers and sprinklers that hit the boards. North Texas also has mold peaking from July through late summer, according to Children's Health.

Washing removes the gray layer and the growth. It takes off a little soft wood fiber with it, which is why pressure matters so much. Cedar and pine do not forgive the setting used on a driveway.

What washing can and can not do

Washing can

  • Take off mildew, algae and gray surface wood
  • Prepare boards for stain or sealer
  • Show you which pickets are actually failing

Washing can not

  • Save rotted pickets or posts
  • Straighten a leaning fence
  • Remove deep black stains from every board
  • Stop mildew from returning where shade stays

What the Dallas code expects from a fence

A clean fence is a choice. A sound fence is a rule. Under Chapter 27 of the Dallas City Code, property owners must keep fences in compliance with set standards.

  • Rotted, fire-damaged or broken wooden slats and support posts must be repaired or replaced.
  • A fence more than 4 feet tall may not lean more than 1 foot from vertical.
  • A fence under 4 feet may not lean more than 6 inches.

So if a company finds soft pickets while washing, that is useful news. Fix those first. Suburban HOAs often have their own fence rules, so check yours before you stain a new color.

Clay soil, posts and the bottom of the fence

The ground matters here too. Clay that swells and shrinks works posts loose, and the lowest boards sit in splashback from sprinklers and rain. Those bottom rows are often the first to rot, so ask the company to keep the spray away from soft spots rather than digging them out further.

Before the visit, trim back climbing plants, clear items along the fence line and turn off sprinklers for the day. Then plan a dry spell, because any stain needs dry wood. Fences take the same care as decks, and the cost guide compares fence prices with other jobs.

Questions to ask about your fence

  • What pressure and tip will you use, and will you test a hidden board first?
  • Is a cleaning product involved, and how will my grass and shrubs be protected?
  • Will you clean both sides, and is the neighbor's side included?
  • What should I do about boards that look soft?

Vinyl and iron fences need a different plan from wood. Ask about those by name. Metal fences may need rust dealt with first, and a vinyl fence usually needs a gentle wash, not force.
