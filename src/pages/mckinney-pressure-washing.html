Two McKinneys, two ways to wash

Power washing in McKinney depends first on which side of town you are in.

Around the square, the city has a Historic Overlay District, and homes and commercial buildings there can be a century old. Out along the newer subdivisions, walls are stone, brick and fiber cement, and the worry is more about maker limits and HOA rules. A company has to treat the two very differently.

Old masonry is the clear case. The Secretary of the Interior's Standards list high-pressure water as not recommended for historic masonry, since it can damage the surface and mortar joints. They prefer low-pressure water, mild detergent and soft brushes, and they say to test a small area first.

Historic Overlay District: what the city says

Generally no approval needed

The city's FAQ says ordinary upkeep that does not change a property's structure, materials or features is not restricted. Painting, repairs and like-for-like replacement typically need no permission.

Talk to the office first

A Certificate of Appropriateness is required for many exterior changes visible from the street. Painting unpainted stucco or masonry is discouraged. If a wash is part of a bigger project, call 972-547-2000 first.

Wilson Creek starts at your curb

McKinney is plain about its waterways. Its Pollution Sources page lists Wilson Creek and the East Fork Trinity River as impaired by bacteria.

Pet waste

The city estimates about 53,000 dogs in McKinney. Scoop before a wash, or the rinse carries it to the curb.

Yard waste

Leaves and grass clog drains and feed algae blooms in creeks. Blow them clear before water starts.

Sediment

Soil washed off a yard clouds creek water. Keep the spray off bare ground.

Newer McKinney homes

Away from the square, the questions change from history to maker limits.

Most of what applies in Frisco applies here too. Fiber cement has its published 1500 psi limit, your HOA may have rules on fence stain, and a slab poured on Collin County clay will move with the seasons. Pale brick and stone show pollen, and Children's Health lists tree pollen here from February to June.

Decide whether your job is house washing, concrete cleaning or a roof soft wash, or all three, then call. The company hears a short summary, so say the important things up front.

Where cleaning products should end up

The city's Pollution Sources page notes that lawn chemicals can wash into storm drains from rain or overwatering, and it points residents to its Household Hazardous Waste collection service for safe disposal of chemicals. The same thinking applies to what is left after an exterior clean. Leftover cleaner should go home with the company, or to a proper disposal point, not down a curb inlet.

On a historic home, ask about testing first. A small trial patch on a back wall shows what the method does to old mortar or paint. On a newer home, the test is just as quick and may save a stone accent or a stained door. Either way, keep the trial out of the rain and look at it once it has dried, since damage often shows up only then.
