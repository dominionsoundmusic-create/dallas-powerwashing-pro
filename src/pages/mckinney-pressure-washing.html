McKinney pressure washing, old town and new town

McKinney is the seat of Collin County and its third-largest city, after Plano and Frisco. The 2020 census counted 195,308 people, up from 131,117 in 2010, and the Census Bureau ranked it the fourth fastest-growing large city in the country from 2010 to 2019. At its center is the square around the Old Collin County Courthouse, now the McKinney Performing Arts Center. Around the edges are new subdivisions and the Craig Ranch area, whose TPC course has hosted the AT&T Byron Nelson since 2020.

Those two sides of McKinney ask for different things from a pressure washing company. Older homes and storefronts near downtown need a light touch and an eye on preservation rules. Newer homes need the usual North Texas care for stone, brick, fences and drives. This free line connects you with an independent, locally owned company for either. You call, you are transferred, and the company quotes, cleans and is paid by you.

The historic district: what McKinney's preservation office says

  • All proposed changes to the exterior of a property in the district need review by the Historic Preservation Office.
  • Routine upkeep generally does not need approval if materials and design stay the same, but check with the office first.
  • Painting unpainted brick or stone is strongly discouraged.
  • To remove paint from masonry, avoid abrasive methods such as sandblasting and use gentler techniques.
  • New fences, or changes to a fence's style, size or location, need a Certificate of Appropriateness.

For washing, that adds up to simple advice: keep it gentle, keep it cleaning rather than changing anything, and call the office at the number on its FAQ page if you are unsure.

Water in McKinney

McKinney is one of the original cities that formed the North Texas Municipal Water District in 1951. In a drought, the district and its member cities can restrict outdoor water use. If restrictions are announced, check the City of McKinney's current rules before booking, and expect a careful company to keep rinse water off the street.
