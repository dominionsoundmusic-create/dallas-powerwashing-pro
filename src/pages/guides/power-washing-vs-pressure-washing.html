The difference between power washing and pressure washing is heated water. A power washer heats the water before it leaves the wand; a pressure washer sprays it cold. Soft washing is the third method, and on a house it is often the most important one.

This guide comes from Dallas Power Washing Pro, a free phone line that connects people across Dallas-Fort Worth with independent local pressure washing companies. The line does not do the cleaning, but it hears the question constantly: which one do I need?

NEXT Insurance's explainer says the heated water in a power washer is preferred for heavily soiled jobs with mildew, salt or grease and is particularly suited to concrete. Angi's cost guide notes that hot water power washing costs more than cold water pressure washing. Fixr describes soft washing as a low-pressure method often recommended for delicate surfaces such as cedar and asphalt shingles.

What soft washing is, and why it exists

Soft washing answers a simple problem: the surfaces people most want clean, the walls and roof of a house, are the ones high pressure damages. A soft wash applies a cleaning solution at low pressure, gives it time to kill mildew and algae and loosen dirt, then rinses gently.

The groups that set care rules for building materials back that up. The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association says not to use a power washer on a roof at all. The Brick Industry Association says high-pressure water cleaning is not appropriate for brick. Trex limits how its newer composite boards may be pressure washed and does not recommend it on early-generation boards.

Which method for which surface around a DFW home

  • Brick, stone and stucco walls: soft wash or low pressure with a masonry-safe cleaner, test patch first.
  • Siding, trim and soffits: soft wash, top down, never spraying up under laps.
  • Asphalt shingle roof: soft wash only.
  • Concrete driveway and walks: pressure washing with a surface cleaner; hot water for oil.
  • Pool deck coating: lower pressure suited to the coating.
  • Wood fence and deck: wood cleaner and gentle, low rinse; brightener before stain.
  • Composite deck: the brand's own rules; often soap, water and a soft brush.
  • Restaurant pad, drive-thru, dumpster area: power washing with hot water and a degreaser, with runoff collected.
