How often to pressure wash a house in Dallas-Fort Worth is less about the calendar and more about your particular house: how much shade it gets, what the walls are made of, how close the trees are, and what the last storm did. Once a year is a sensible baseline. Here is how North Texas seasons shift it.

This guide comes from Dallas Power Washing Pro, a free phone line that connects homeowners with independent local pressure washing companies. It does not clean houses itself, and nothing here replaces the judgment of a company that has seen your walls.

The baseline: once a year, adjusted for your house

Thumbtack's guide to pressure washing gives a simple rule: wash the house at least once a year, and more often if you get a lot of rain, wind and storms. DFW gets plenty of the last two. Use these adjustments:

How house conditions change how often to wash

Full sun, brick, few treesEvery 1 to 2 yearsDust and pollen, but little growth

Siding, painted trim or stuccoEvery yearLight surfaces and texture show dirt and growth sooner
Near a busy road or constructionEvery year or as neededDust and road grime build up fast
About to paint, sell or listRight beforePaint needs a clean surface; photos need curb appeal

These intervals are rules of thumb, not standards. The only real test is what the walls look like.

A North Texas year, season by season

Late winter to spring: pollen

NBC 5's weather team lists elm pollen from February into late March, mulberry from mid-February to mid-April, and oak pollen in late winter and spring, which it describes as the yellow dust on homes, cars and patios. Washing after the worst of it has fallen keeps the house clean longer.

Spring: storms and hail

Spring storms drive grit, leaves and mud onto walls and walks. Insurance Council of Texas figures reported in 2012 counted roughly 1,350 hailstorms in the immediate Dallas-Fort Worth area since 1955. After a big storm, check for damage before you wash.

Summer: heat

The National Weather Service puts DFW's average first 100 degree day near July 1 and the last near August 27. Washing is fine in summer, but cleaning solutions dry fast in the sun, so good companies work in the shade or the cooler hours.

Winter: freezes

North Texas freezes are usually short, but not always. In February 2021, DFW Airport spent 139 straight hours at or below freezing and hit 2 degrees below zero. Wash water left on walks and steps before a freeze turns to ice, so winter washing waits for a mild stretch.

Signs it is time, whatever the calendar says

  • Green or black film on the north wall, under eaves or behind shrubs
  • Dark streaks below windows or downspouts
  • Yellow pollen or gray dust you can wipe off with a finger
  • Spider webs and mud dauber nests in the corners of the eaves
  • Chalky residue on painted siding that rubs off on your hand
  • You are about to paint, stain, sell or host an event

The rest of the property

  • Driveways and walks: when the shaded edges go gray-green or oil builds up, often every year or two.
  • Roofs: when streaks show. ARMA notes cleaning is temporary and algae can return.
  • Wood fences and decks: before each re-stain, and when boards turn green or slick.
  • Composite decks: Trex recommends spring and fall cleaning for its early-generation boards to keep pollen and debris from feeding mold.

Before painting: wash first, then wait

If the reason for washing is a paint job, Sherwin-Williams recommends pressure washing to remove dirt, mildew and loose paint, then letting the surface dry for at least 24 to 48 hours before painting. Tell the washing company that paint is next.
