Do you need a license to pressure wash in Texas? Not from the state. There is no Texas pressure washing license and no general state business license. But a company that washes properties in Dallas-Fort Worth still answers to city storm water rules, a Fort Worth permit for mobile washers, and state sales tax.

This guide is written for homeowners and business owners checking out a company, not as legal advice. It comes from Dallas Power Washing Pro, a free phone line that connects callers with independent local pressure washing companies. The line itself holds no license, certification or bond, and does no cleaning.

What the state requires (and does not)

No general business license

The Office of the Texas Governor's Business Permits Office says: "The State of Texas does not require a general license." Businesses register with the Texas Secretary of State or a county clerk instead, depending on how they are set up.

Not a TDLR program

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation licenses dozens of occupations, from electricians and AC technicians to mold assessors and remediators. Pressure washing and exterior cleaning are not on its list.

One related line is worth knowing. Mold assessment and mold remediation are licensed TDLR programs in Texas. Washing mildew off the outside of a house is not mold remediation, and a pressure washing company should not present it as such. If you have mold inside, that is a different call. The same phone number takes mold calls for the Dallas area.

Fort Worth: a permit for mobile power washers

Fort Worth is the clearest local rule in DFW. The city requires a permit for "any person or business who engages in mobile commercial cosmetic cleaning." The permit is $25 for the business, plus a $25 registration certificate for each wash unit. It lasts one year, cannot be transferred, and every vehicle and trailer used must be registered with its number displayed.

Wash water that does not qualify for an exemption must be collected before it leaves the property, any water that does reach the storm drain must first pass through an oil absorbent boom or pad, and the city says permit violations can bring fines of up to $2,000 per occurrence. Businesses that power wash their own property with their own staff do not need the permit but must follow the same discharge rules.

Wash water and storm drains across DFW

Storm drains in North Texas run to creeks and lakes untreated. The regional council of governments' stormwater FAQ says no local government in North Central Texas treats stormwater. That is why cities regulate wash water even where they do not license the washer.

  • Dallas: the city tells businesses that letting soapy or polluted water reach the storm drain violates city code and the federal Clean Water Act. Plain water may drain only if it is uncontaminated, harmless to the creek and causes no erosion. With chemicals, block the drain and collect the water.
  • Fort Worth: the city's FAQ says discharging any type of wastewater off-property has been illegal since the turn of the century.
  • Denton: city code section 26-155(d) prohibits discharging pollutants to the storm sewer without treatment or a permit, with fines of up to $2,000 per violation per day, according to the city.

Other cities have similar ordinances. A homeowner hiring a company is unlikely to be the one fined, but it is a good test of the company: ask how it keeps wash water out of the drain.

Sales tax on pressure washing

The Texas Comptroller's guide to cleaning and janitorial services says pressure washing buildings, sidewalks and parking lots is taxable as building or grounds cleaning, and pressure washing equipment or other property is taxable as maintenance of tangible personal property. It lists cleaning a home among taxable building cleaning charges.

For a homeowner that simply means a quote may add sales tax. Ask whether the number you are given includes it. Questions about your own tax situation belong with the Comptroller.

What to check instead of a license

  • Proof of liability insurance, with the dates and limits.
  • A written price for each surface, and whether tax is included.
  • The method for each surface: soft wash for walls, roofs and wood.
  • Runoff control: drain covers, booms, recovery.
  • The Fort Worth permit, for work in Fort Worth.
