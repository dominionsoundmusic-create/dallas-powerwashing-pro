Roof cleaning in Dallas is almost always about dark streaks: long black or brown stains running down from the ridge, usually worst on the shady slopes. They are algae, they make a house look older than it is, and the one thing the roofing industry agrees on is that you should never blast them off.

This is a free phone line that connects you with a company that does the work; it does not clean roofs itself. An automated assistant answers any hour, asks about the roof and the house, and transfers you to an independent, locally owned DFW company that soft washes roofs. That company inspects, quotes, cleans and is paid by you.

What the shingle makers say

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association, the trade group for the companies that make asphalt shingles, publishes guidance on algae streaks. In short:

It is algae

Gloeocapsa magma is probably the most common of several algae species that cause the discoloration, and its pigment turns light roofs dark over time.

It can be lightened

ARMA says the discoloration may be lightened with a solution of household bleach and water, and that the effect is temporary.

Never power wash

ARMA says not to use a power washer, brush or broom on the roof surface, because high-pressure washing is likely to damage asphalt roofing.

That is the case for a soft wash. ARMA warns that high-pressure washing is likely to damage asphalt roofing, and that even scrubbing can loosen and remove the mineral granules on the shingle surface, which help shield the asphalt from the sun. On a roof that already takes North Texas summers, where the Weather Service records an average of about two months between the first and last 100 degree day, losing granules early is the last thing you want.

What a roof soft wash looks like

The company applies a cleaning solution to the shingles at low pressure, lets it work on the algae, and rinses gently. Gutters and downspouts carry the runoff, so a careful crew protects the beds and lawn under them and rinses plants before and after.

Ask the company what to expect: streaks may not look fully gone the moment it leaves, and some may keep lightening as the treated growth weathers off. A company should tell you that up front instead of scrubbing hard to make it look perfect by the time it leaves, since ARMA warns scrubbing can strip granules.

Hail first, cleaning second

DFW gets a lot of hail. A 2012 CBS Texas report on Insurance Council of Texas figures put Tarrant County at the top of the state's county list, citing National Weather Service records of 800 hailstorms there since 1955. If a storm has just come through, a stained roof might also be a damaged one. A cleaning company is not the right party to judge that.

Questions worth asking any roof cleaning company

  • Will you soft wash, and will anyone use a pressure washer on the shingles? (The answer to the second should be no.)
  • How will you get on the roof, and do you carry liability insurance?
  • How will you protect my plants, lawn and anything under the downspouts?
  • What should I expect to see right after, and over the next week?
  • Is the price written, and does it include the gutters' outside faces?

If you are replacing a roof soon, ARMA notes that algae-resistant shingles are designed to inhibit growth for an extended time. That is a question for the roofer, not the cleaner.
