What those streaks on a Dallas roof really are

Roof cleaning in Dallas usually starts with a homeowner noticing dark stripes running down the shady side.

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association says the discoloration is mainly algae, Gloeocapsa magma, which looks brown to black and is often mistaken for soot or dirt. It is most common where conditions are warm and humid. Algae spores travel in the air, so it can spread from roof to roof. Bob Vila's guide notes that stains tend to start on north-facing slopes and under heavy tree cover.

A humid Dallas summer, a live oak over the house and a north slope is about the right recipe. The streaks are mostly a cosmetic problem, though moisture held in the growth can age shingles early.

What ARMA asks for, in plain words

The trade group's guidance gives a good checklist for any company you hire. ARMA recommends trained professionals over homeowners doing this work.

  • Apply the solution gently, with no scrubbing, since friction can loosen granules.
  • Rinse gently with water after 15 to 20 minutes.
  • Use no other cleaning chemicals without the shingle maker's approval.
  • Expect the roof to be slippery during treatment. That is one strong reason to leave it to a pro.

Plants, drains and the rinse

Many roof cleaners use bleach, and bleach runoff is hard on landscaping. Bob Vila's guide says to cover plants with tarps. Ask the company how it will protect beds below the eaves, and how it will keep runoff from the downspouts out of the street. A solution that reaches a storm drain breaks the same city rules that apply to any soapy wash water.

Also ask about tile and metal roofs, which need a different plan. The photo at the top of this page shows a tile-style roof. Do not assume that advice for asphalt fits a tile roof, or the reverse.
