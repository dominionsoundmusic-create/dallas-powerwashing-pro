Oak shade is the Flower Mound problem

If you are looking at power washing in Flower Mound, the first thing to look at may be your trees.

The town's Master Plan describes a largely intact Cross Timbers area in central Flower Mound and a Cross Timbers Conservation Development District to protect it. The Cross Timbers is the band of oak woodland that the Handbook of Texas traces through eastern Denton County. Texas Parks and Wildlife names post oak, blackjack oak and cedar elm as its trees, on slightly acidic sandy or sandy loam soils. Compared with a bare prairie subdivision, a wooded Flower Mound lot gets more shade, more leaf litter and walls that stay damp for longer.

That is good for your summer electric bill, and it is also how green walls, slick driveways and streaked roofs begin. Bob Vila's roof guide points to the same pattern, with stains starting on north slopes and under heavy tree cover.

Flower Mound's water rules are stricter in a drought

In many nearby cities, the ban on washing surfaces comes at Stage 3. The Flower Mound ordinance this site read puts it at Stage 2.

Ordinance 19-19 says Stage 2 will "prohibit hosing off of paved areas, buildings, windows, and other surfaces," and later stages keep that rule. The town's current long-term water plan is described under a newer ordinance, so the stage details may have been updated. If the town has declared a stage, check before you book, because that wash may have to wait. Call the town at 972-874-6000.

In normal conditions, the two-day schedule and the 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. limit apply to sprinklers and hose-end watering, and hand-held hose use on landscaped areas is allowed at any time.

Getting a wooded lot ready

Rake or blow leaves off the drive and away from inlets before work starts. Wet leaves stain concrete and clog drains. Trim back limbs touching walls and roof edges, since regrowth starts at those contact points. Ask the company to keep cleaning products off roots and beds, and to rinse plants if any drift reaches them.

Then choose a dry stretch with leaves mostly down, so the clean lasts. Read the how often guide for timing on a shaded home.

HOA lots, watering days and shared walks

Flower Mound's schedule treats HOA lots, common areas and commercial sites differently from homes. Residential addresses have their own two days, while non-residential, HOA and multi-family properties water on Monday and Thursday. That matters if you manage a community. Book exterior washing away from sprinkler days, or the first thing on a clean wall may be muddy overspray.

Home owners with a bigger wooded lot can also ask about working in stages: roof and walls one visit, drive and walks the next, once the debris from the first has been cleared. A hand-held hose for watering landscaped areas is allowed at any time under the town's plan, so if a little cleaner does reach plants, a rinse is usually easy. Ask your company which products it uses, and how they affect oak roots.
