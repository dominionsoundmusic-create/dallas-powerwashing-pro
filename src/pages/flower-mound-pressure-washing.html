Pressure washing in the Town of Flower Mound

Flower Mound calls itself a town, though under Texas law it is a city, and with 75,956 residents at the 2020 census it is the largest Texas municipality using the "town" label. It sits almost entirely in Denton County beside Grapevine Lake, in the Eastern Cross Timbers, and takes its name from "The Mound," a 12.5-acre hill of prairie grasses and wildflowers near FM 3040 and FM 2499. Since 1999 its SMARTGrowth program has managed the pace and character of development, and the town encourages conservation development to protect open space and trees.

That character shapes the cleaning many homes here need: long driveways, long fence lines, decks under big trees, and roofs and walls that stay shaded. This free line connects you with an independent, locally owned company for it. You call, you are transferred, and the company quotes, cleans and is paid by you.

Trees, shade and the dark side of the house

Cross Timbers trees are lovely and they keep things damp. Shaded roofs streak with algae, which the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association says should be soft washed and never power washed. Fences and decks under the canopy gray and grow mildew. Family Handyman's advice for wood is gentle pressure, a good cleaner and at least three dry days before stain.

A larger lot usually means a longer driveway and more concrete to clean, which affects the price. Tell the assistant roughly how long the drive is and how many feet of fence you have.

Check the water stage first

Flower Mound uses a staged emergency water demand plan. In July 2018, for example, the town moved to Stage 1 "water awareness" after little rain and high temperatures, limiting automatic sprinklers to two days a week and banning them from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Stages change from year to year. Before booking a big wash in a dry summer, check the town's current stage, and expect a careful company to ask about it too.
