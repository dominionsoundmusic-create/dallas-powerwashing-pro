Deck cleaning starts with knowing the boards

The first question for deck cleaning is what the deck is made of.

Many wood decks are softwood such as pine or cedar. Composite boards look like wood but follow a different rule book, set by their maker. If you do not know which you have, a company should be able to tell by looking, and you can check your paperwork for the brand and year.

The damage from too much pressure is called etching. It shows as stripes or fuzzy grooves that follow the path of the wand. HGTV warns that holding the spray in one spot can gash wood permanently.

A sensible order for wood decks

The steps below are what the published guides describe. A company may change the order after seeing your deck.

  1. Clear and checkMove furniture and grills, and look for loose boards and raised nail heads.
  2. Test a hidden spotHGTV suggests a stair tread or another low-visibility spot before moving to the main boards.
  3. Wash with the grainSteady distance, wand always moving, along the length of each board.
  4. Let it dryStain needs dry wood, so a wet week means waiting.
  5. Sand and stain if neededSand raised fibers first, then seal.

Timing a deck job in North Texas

Dallas-Fort Worth gets most of its rain in spring and fall, according to the National Weather Service. Staining right before a stormy week wastes money, and summer heat dries stain quickly on boards in full sun. Mild, dry stretches are the easy target. Ask the company what it would choose for your deck.

Under the deck matters as well. Wash water carries dirt and old stain flakes, so cover anything below and keep the runoff off the street. If you have a wood fence nearby, doing both on one visit may make sense. Check the how often guide for a sensible schedule.

What a deck wash can not fix

A clean deck can still be a weak one.

Washing will not bring back a board that has split, cupped or gone soft, and it can make a weak board look worse by clearing away what was hiding the damage. Look for dark soft spots near posts and stairs, and press them with a screwdriver. Tell the company what you find. Repairs come first, then cleaning, then stain.

Also think about what is below. On a raised deck, dirty water drips through the gaps onto whatever sits underneath, so cover it or move it. On a ground-level deck, runoff goes straight to the soil beside the house, so ask how cleaning products will be kept away from plants.
