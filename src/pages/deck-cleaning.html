Deck cleaning in Dallas covers two very different materials. Wood decks need a gentle wash that does not splinter the boards and leaves them ready for stain. Composite decks need a cleaning that stays inside the manufacturer's rules, because the warranty depends on it. Both collect North Texas pollen, leaves and mildew in the gaps.

Wood or composite: two different jobs

Wood decks

Pine, cedar and other deck woods are soft. Family Handyman warns that a pressure washer on a high setting can damage or scar a wood deck, and that cleaning can lift the wood fibers. The usual approach is a wood cleaner, a low, wide rinse that follows the grain, and often a brightener before stain.

  • Expect some raised grain; light sanding may be needed before staining
  • Plan for several dry days before stain goes on
  • Old, failing stain may need a stripper, which is a separate step

Composite decks

Composite boards are not wood, and each brand publishes its own care rules. Trex, for example, allows a gentle pressure wash on its newer lines only within its pressure limit and with a fan tip at least 8 inches away, warns that going past that can void the warranty, and does not recommend pressure washing its early-generation boards at all.

  • Find the brand and line, or ask the company to identify it
  • Soap, water and a soft brush handle a lot of composite cleaning
  • No stain needed, but mold spots need the food source removed

Covered patios, pergolas and outdoor kitchens

A lot of DFW backyards have a covered patio off the house instead of, or as well as, a deck: a concrete or stone slab under a roof, a pergola, an outdoor kitchen with a grill. The slab is a hard surface and can usually take a surface cleaner. The ceiling, beams, fans and light fixtures above it need care, and grease around the grill needs a degreaser.

Tell the company about the patio cover, any fans or lights, cushions and rugs, and whether there is a stone or tile floor. That shapes the quote and the method.

What a good deck cleaning visit includes

  1. Identify the boardsWood species, or composite brand and line, plus any existing stain or sealer.
  2. Clear and protectFurniture off, plants below and around wet down, nearby siding and windows rinsed.
  3. Clean the gapsLeaves and dirt between boards hold water and feed mildew.
  4. Apply the right cleanerWood cleaner or composite-safe soap, with time to work.
  5. Rinse gentlyLow pressure or soft brush, following the boards, never stopping in one spot.
  6. Brighten wood if stainingAnd agree on drying time before any finish goes on.

North Texas timing

Spring is when DFW decks look worst and when people most want to use them. NBC 5's weather team describes oak pollen as the yellow dust that coats homes, cars and patios in late winter and spring. Cleaning after the heaviest pollen drop means the deck stays clean longer. If you plan to stain a wood deck, pick a stretch of dry days, and avoid the hottest part of summer when stain can dry too fast.
