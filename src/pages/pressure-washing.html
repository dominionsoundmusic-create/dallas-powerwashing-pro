Surface by surface: what a good company changes

"Pressure washing" is really a set of methods. The right one depends on what the surface is made of. Here is how the common surfaces around a Texas home are usually handled.

The Brick Industry Association's guidance on cleaning brickwork says high-pressure water cleaning is not appropriate for brick and recommends trying a small test area first and letting it dry before judging the result. That habit, a test patch before the whole job, is a good sign in any company.

Why North Texas concrete gets dirty fast

Much of DFW sits on dark clay that swells when it is wet and shrinks when it dries. The Dallas County Master Gardeners describe that clay pulling away and cracking in summer and heaving when it is soaked. Patios and walks ride those movements, so they develop hairline cracks and slight lips at the joints, and every crack holds dirt and moisture.

Add oak pollen in spring, which NBC 5's weather team describes as the yellow dust that coats homes and patios, plus sprinkler overspray and summer heat that bakes grime on, and a light-colored slab can look gray within a year or two.

When you might need pressure washing near you

  • Before selling or listing a home. Clean walks and patios show up in every listing photo.
  • Before painting or staining. Paint makers say to wash first; Sherwin-Williams suggests letting the surface dry 24 to 48 hours before painting.
  • After a storm. Spring hail and wind leave grit, shredded leaves and mud splashed across hardscape.
  • When a walk turns slick. Green growth on shaded concrete can make steps slippery when wet.
  • Ahead of an HOA inspection. Many neighborhood associations send notices for stained walks and drives. Check your deed restrictions for what they require.

The real dangers of pressure washing a home

Most of the harm comes from pressure used where it does not belong. Water forced under lap siding or around window frames can soak insulation and framing. Mortar joints on brick can wash out. Wood gets furry and gouged. Shingles lose their granules. And on the safety side, ladders, wet surfaces and a wand that kicks back are a poor mix for someone doing it for the first time. That is the case for calling a company that does this every week and can show proof of insurance.

Before washing, a good company will also ask what you have used on the surface before. Sealers, paints and old stain treatments change what cleaner is safe to use.

Where the rinse water goes

Storm drains in North Texas are not treated. The regional council of governments' stormwater FAQ notes that no local government in North Central Texas treats stormwater, so whatever goes down the curb inlet reaches the nearest creek.

The City of Dallas tells property owners that wash water may only go to the street or storm drain if it is plain water with no soap, degreaser or other chemical, does not harm the creek, and does not cause erosion or muddy runoff. If cleaning chemicals are used, Dallas says to block the storm drain and collect the contaminated water. Ask the company how it handles runoff on your street.
