What residential pressure washing covers

House washing in Dallas usually means the outside walls, soffits, trim, porch and entry, with the walks around them rinsed afterward.

The dirt on a Dallas home tends to be a mix. Pollen sits on ledges for much of the year. Green growth takes hold on walls that stay shaded and damp. Mud splashes up along the foundation after spring storms. Each one comes off differently, and strong pressure is rarely the best answer to any of them.

A careful company starts by walking the house. It looks for loose paint, failed caulk, cracked mortar and gaps around windows, because water finds those first.

Why heat matters on a Texas wall

Angi's guidance warns that heat and direct sun can cause stains and streaks and weaken cleaning products. In Dallas, that shapes the whole summer.

  1. Work the shady side firstA company will often follow the shade around the house, so cleaner never dries on hot brick.
  2. Start early in summerThe National Weather Service says July and August highs often reach 100 degrees here. Early hours give cleaners time to work.
  3. Think about springSpring is the most recommended season, but thunderstorms peak then too, and mud can splash back on a clean wall.

Getting the house ready

  • Close windows and doors, and check that they latch.
  • Move furniture, grills and pots away from the walls.
  • Tell the company about outdoor outlets, lights and meters.
  • Point out any window seal or caulk that has failed.
  • Water your plants first if a cleaning product is planned, and ask how they will be protected.

Soapy runoff is a separate issue. In Dallas, plain water from washing a driveway or structure has a defense under city code, while water with soap or similar cleaners does not. Ask where the water will go. See the how often guide for timing, and the roof page if streaks reach the shingles.

Residential pressure washing versus doing it yourself

A rented machine looks easy on a sidewalk. A wall is harder, because the risks sit above your head and behind the surface. Water driven under siding can stay there, and a mistake on old paint can spread lead dust. If you do clean at ground level yourself, use a wide tip, stay well back, and stop if the surface changes color or texture. Leave anything that needs a ladder to someone else.
