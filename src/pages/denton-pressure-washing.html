Pressure washing in Denton means thinking downstream

Denton's own watershed page puts it plainly: storm drains here lead to Lewisville Lake, a primary drinking water source for much of the Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex.

The city calls everyday runoff from homes, yards and cars the leading cause of water quality decline in Denton. Water from a home wash job usually follows the same path, down the driveway, along the curb and into an inlet. So a question that sounds fussy elsewhere has a clear answer here. Ask where the water will go, and expect a real plan if soap is involved.

Denton lists a manual titled BMPs for Pressure Washing and Surface Cleaning. Ask a company that works here whether it knows it.

Oak shade and sandy ground

Denton sits where two landscapes meet, and the Handbook of Texas places the Eastern Cross Timbers across the eastern part of the county.

Under the post oaks

Texas Parks and Wildlife lists post oak, blackjack oak and cedar elm in the East Cross Timbers. Tree-shaded north walls and fences hold moisture, so green growth comes back sooner here than on an open lot.

Soil that changes

The same source describes slightly acidic sandy or sandy loam soils in the East Cross Timbers. To the east, heavy prairie clay begins. Do not assume every Denton slab moves the same way. Ask the company to look at yours.

What Denton owners usually call about

  • Older homes near downtown: check the build year. Paint on a home built before 1978 may contain lead.
  • Rentals near campus: walks and front walls before a new lease.
  • Fences under trees: mildew on the shaded side, gray on the sunny side.
  • Shops and restaurants: entries and dumpster pads, where wash water needs the most care.
  • Roofs under oaks: streaks on shaded slopes, which call for a soft wash, not high pressure.

A simple plan for a Denton job

Clear leaves and yard debris off the drive first, since Denton lists mowing among the everyday habits that can clog or pollute a storm drain. Block the nearest inlet if the job calls for cleaner. Keep runoff on the grass where you can. If the wash uses only water, Dallas's guidance to divert the flow to grass works just as well in Denton.

What triggers a Denton drought stage

Denton's drought plan ties its stages to three things: how much water is stored in the lakes, how high demand runs compared with what the system can deliver, and emergencies such as line breaks or contamination. So a stage can arrive quickly in a hot summer, and it may not mean the lakes are low. If you book a big exterior job weeks ahead, check again a few days before.
