Pressure washing in Denton, from the Square to the lake

Denton is the seat of Denton County, with 139,869 people in the 2020 census. It is a university town twice over: the University of North Texas enrolls more than 42,000 students and Texas Woman's University, founded in 1901, more than 13,000. At its heart is the Courthouse-on-the-Square, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, with shops and restaurants on the streets around it. The town also calls itself the Redbud Capital of Texas.

Denton was incorporated in 1866, so alongside newer subdivisions it has older houses with wood porches and siding, rentals near the two campuses, and storefronts around a historic square. Denton's storm drains run to the drinking water

The City of Denton says its storm drains lead to Lewisville Lake, which it calls a primary source of drinking water for much of the Dallas-Fort Worth area, and it monitors water near its own intake on the lake. Pecan Creek, Cooper Creek, Hickory Creek and Clear Creek carry the city's runoff.

The city defines an illicit discharge as anything going to the storm system that is not entirely stormwater, and lists car wash wastewater among the common sources. City code section 26-155(d) prohibits discharging pollutants to the storm sewer without treatment or a permit, with fines that can reach $2,000 per violation per day. Ask any company how it keeps wash water and cleaners out of the drain.

Watch the water stage before you book

Denton draws its water from Lewisville Lake and Ray Roberts Lake. Its 2019 drought contingency plan treats hosing down surfaces in steps:

Denton drought plan rules on hosing off surfaces
StageWhat the plan says about hosing off surfaces
Stage 1 (mild)Recommends customers do not hose off paved areas, buildings, windows or other surfaces
Stage 2 (moderate)Prohibits hosing off paved areas, buildings, windows or other surfaces

This is not theoretical. In August 2024, Denton moved to Stage 2 and once-a-week outdoor watering after demand exceeded the city's daily treatment capacity three days running while filters at the Lake Lewisville plant were being repaired. If a stage is in effect, ask the city about professional cleaning before you book.
