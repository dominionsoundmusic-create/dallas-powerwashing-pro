Why power washing in Frisco starts with the HOA

Some Frisco homeowners go looking for a washer because of a letter, a sale or a neighbor's clean driveway, and an HOA is often part of that story.

Many Frisco neighborhoods have an HOA that looks after common areas. The city says those associations are often responsible for ponds, creeks and other stormwater features. So on some Frisco streets, wash water in the gutter may be heading for water your own dues help maintain. The city's HOA guidance notes that low oxygen can cause fish kills in these ponds. Detergent running into one is not something any board wants to pay for.

Before you book, check two things with your manager: what your deed restrictions say about fences and stain colors, and whether a shared wall or entry is the HOA's job and not yours.

What Frisco's ordinance says, in plain words

Article IX of the city's code covers stormwater. It is short on washing and clear on the point that matters.

The base rule

Section 34-331 bars any discharge to the storm system that is not entirely stormwater.

Power washers

Section 34-332 lists mobile power washer wastewater with soap or other harmful cleaners, and wastewater from pavement with an unremoved spill.

The penalties

The ordinance allows civil penalties of up to $1,000 a day for most listed matters, higher for discharges, and criminal fines of up to $2,000 per violation per day.

What Frisco homes usually need

Many Frisco homes are fairly new, so lead paint is less of a concern than in older Dallas neighborhoods, though any home built before 1978 still calls for the question. The more usual Frisco questions are about fiber cement, stone and stucco accents, and the maker's limits on each.

Spills, gas stations and business parks

Frisco's ordinance has two clauses that matter for businesses. Pavement where a spill was not removed counts among the wastewater sources people must keep out of the system. And service station pavement wash water must pass through a properly maintained grease, oil and sand interceptor before it enters the drain. So a manager should ask the company to deal with spills first and to explain where wash water goes. If a violation does occur, the ordinance lets the city send a warning notice or a notice of violation, and it also allows the city to recover the cost of responding to a release.
