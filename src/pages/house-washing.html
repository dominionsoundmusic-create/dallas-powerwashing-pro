House washing in Dallas mostly means brick. Brick fronts, stone accents, fiber cement or vinyl on the second story, painted trim and soffits. Each of those reacts differently to water pressure, which is why the right way to wash a house here is a soft wash: low pressure, a cleaning solution, and time for it to work.

Soft washing, in plain terms

A pressure washer cleans by force. A soft wash cleans mostly by chemistry. The company applies a cleaning solution at low pressure, lets it sit long enough to kill mildew and algae and loosen dirt, then rinses from the top down. The wall ends up clean without the jet ever being strong enough to push water into the gaps.

That matters on a house because walls are full of gaps by design: the overlap on lap siding, weep holes along the bottom of a brick wall that let moisture out, joints around windows and doors. A strong jet aimed upward drives water into all of them.

What each wall material needs

Brick and mortar

The Brick Industry Association says high-pressure water cleaning is not appropriate for brick and can erode mortar joints. Expect low pressure, a masonry-safe cleaner and a test spot first.

Stone and cast stone

Softer stones and the trim around windows can etch or stain with the wrong cleaner. A careful company asks what the stone is and tests before washing the whole facade.

Stucco

Textured and prone to holding dirt in its pores. Low pressure and a longer dwell time clean it; a hard jet can chip the finish coat.

Fiber cement and vinyl siding

Cleaned gently from the top down so water is never driven up under the laps. Ask the company whether it follows the siding maker's care instructions.

Painted trim and soffits

Old paint can lift under pressure. If you are about to repaint, say so: a slightly firmer wash may be what you want. If not, keep it soft.

Windows and screens

Window glass usually gets a rinse during a house wash, but detailed window cleaning is a different service. Ask what is included.

Why Dallas houses turn green on one side

Walk around almost any house in DFW and the dirtiest wall is the one that sees the least sun: usually the north side, or any side shaded by a live oak, a neighbor's house or a tall hedge. Morning dew and sprinkler spray dry slowly there, and that slow drying is what mildew and algae need. The south and west walls get the summer heat instead, which bakes dust onto brick and fades paint.

That is why a good house wash is not one uniform pass. The green side gets a stronger treatment and more dwell time; the sunny sides mostly need dust and pollen rinsed off. Ask the company which side it thinks is worst. If it has looked properly, it will know.

House washing services near you: what to ask before you book

  • Will you soft wash the walls? For brick, stucco and siding, the answer should be yes.
  • What will you use, and how will you protect plants? Wetting beds before and after is standard.
  • Can I see proof of liability insurance? Texas does not license pressure washing, so this is your protection.
  • What is included? Walls, eaves, soffits, gutters' outer faces, window rinse, the front porch: get it listed.
  • Where will the rinse water go? The City of Dallas says wash water with soap or chemicals must not reach the storm drain, and North Texas storm drains are not treated.
