The call, one step at a time

Dial {{ site.business.phone_display }} whenever suits you. The line never closes.

  1. An automated assistant answersIt is a shared Dallas line, so it asks what you need. Say you need pressure or power washing.
  2. You say what and whereA house, a driveway, a roof, a fence, a deck or a business, and your city.
  3. Your call is transferredIt goes to an independent local company, which hears a short spoken summary so you do not have to repeat yourself.
  4. If no one answersA message is taken and passed to the company for a call back.
  5. From here on, it is between you and themThe company looks over the job, names its own price, carries out the work and sends you the bill.

Would you rather type? The contact page has a short form, and a company calls you back.

What the line is, and what it is not

The line does

  • Answer your call
  • Ask a few questions
  • Connect you or take a message

The line does not

  • Inspect or quote any job
  • Send workers or equipment
  • Do any cleaning
  • Take payment from you

Questions to ask the company

Texas lists no state license for power washing, so your questions matter more than they would in a licensed trade.

Insurance

Ask for a certificate of insurance before work begins. Ask what happens if something is damaged.

Water

Ask where the wash water goes and how soap stays out of storm drains. In Fort Worth, ask about the city permit.

Method

Ask what pressure, tip and cleaner it will use on each surface, and why.

Business

Ask for the legal name. Look it up on the Texas Comptroller's account status search, which shows if an entity may transact business in Texas.

Paper

Get the price and scope in writing. Door-to-door sales over $25 can usually be cancelled within three business days.

Training

Power Washers of North America is a trade group that offers certification. It is optional. Ask if the company holds it.

Your details

What you tell the assistant, or type in the form, goes to the company that helps you so it can reach you. The privacy policy has the full details, and the terms explain that your agreement is with the company you hire, not the line.

Sole proprietors file assumed names with the county clerk, while companies and LLCs file with the Texas Secretary of State, so a business name can often be traced. Ask if you are not sure who you are dealing with.
