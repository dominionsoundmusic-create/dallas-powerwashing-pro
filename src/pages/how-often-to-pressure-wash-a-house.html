How often to pressure wash a house: the common answer

Most published advice lands in the same place: about once a year.

Angi says about once a year, and more often if you live in a humid area or near leafy trees, sap, pollen or frequent storms. Fixr says professionals generally recommend once a year. Forbes says at least once a year. Thumbtack calls once a year a minimum and adds that weather and climate change the answer.

That last point is the useful one. Almost everything on Angi's list of reasons to wash more often describes North Texas in a normal year. Still, one schedule does not fit every wall on one house, so start by walking around yours.

Read your own walls

Take five minutes on a dry day. These signs say more than a calendar.

  • Green or black tint on north or shaded walls
  • Yellow dust settled on ledges and trim
  • Mud splashed along the base of the walls
  • Dark drip lines under gutters or window sills
  • A chalky feel on old paint or siding

If you see these on only one side, you may be able to wash one side now and leave the rest for later. Ask the company whether it will quote part of the house.

A North Texas year for a house wash

Seasons here are not the ones most national guides assume. Pollen dates come from Children's Health, and weather from the National Weather Service.

Different schedules for different surfaces

A house is many jobs at once. A sensible plan treats them separately.

  • Walls: about once a year, sooner on shaded sides.
  • Driveway and walks: one to two years, or yearly in shade, per HomeGuide.
  • Roof: only when streaks appear, and only with a soft wash.
  • Fence: when mildew or gray boards bother you, and before staining. See fence cleaning.
  • Deck: before you stain or seal. See deck cleaning.

When a drought changes the plan

In a bad drought, the schedule may not be up to you. The plans for Dallas and for the cities supplied by the North Texas Municipal Water District ban hosing paved areas and buildings at Stage 3, except by variance and by a professional service using high efficiency equipment, though the district's member cities adopt and enforce these rules themselves. Fort Worth's plan discourages hosing at Stage 1, and it limits pressure washing at Stage 3. The versions this site read for Flower Mound and Denton prohibit hosing off paved areas and buildings at Stage 2, and those may have changed.

This site does not track the current stage for each city. Call your water utility before you book. The cost guide covers what a wash runs, and you can compare what you see against the services page.

How often for a house you are selling or painting

Two events can override any schedule. Thumbtack notes that people often wash before repainting or to help a home show well for buyers. Before paint, ask your painter how long the surface should dry. Before a sale, wash a week or two ahead so any fresh mud or pollen can be rinsed before photos. Fort Worth's drought plan keeps an exception for preparing a surface for paint, which shows how routine that step is, but other cities' plans may differ.
